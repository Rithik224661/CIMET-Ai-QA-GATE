00 · How to read thisGate logic & coverage

What the gate does, and where each capability appears

Gate logic
  • 01All critical checks pass → AUTO-SUBMIT, no human touch.
  • 02Any critical fail → HOLD, routed to the TL queue with the failing line, transcript and timestamp.
  • 03Low confidence on any check → QA REVIEW. Nothing uncertain auto-passes.
  • 045% of clean calls are sampled to a human anyway — the model is measured, not trusted.
  • 05Every score resolves to the check-library version live on the call date.
  • 06A human override is logged beside the AI decision, never in place of it.
Where to look
  • ▸Scoring accuracy — Calibration: agreement, false-pass and false-fail counts.
  • ▸Coverage — Full checklist: 20 checks across Verbatim, Factual and Behaviour for Retailer 1's energy list.
  • ▸Traceability — Evidence inspector and Audit ledger: transcript line, timestamp, rule version, decision lineage.
  • ▸Gate & escalation — Decision header, QA queue, QA-review and processing states.
  • ▸Guardrails — Low-confidence routing on crosstalk, redaction notice on playback, no auto-correction.

01 · DashboardOperational control surface

Executive QA overview

Operational health first, then decision distribution, then the work itself. Which check is failing answers the brief's "critical fail rate, and which specific check" directly; repeat offences surface agents failing the same critical three or more times in a rolling seven days.
02 · QA / TL queuePrioritised work, not a record list

Work queue

Filtered by the reason a human is needed: critical holds, low confidence, repeat offences, human overrides, sampled clean calls. Confidence shows the deciding check, so the number explains the routing; unscored leads show "—" rather than a fabricated score.
03 · Decision workspaceLead 3613790 · Retailer 1 · 30 min call

The decision is the largest object on the page

The worked example from the brief. HOLD, the reason, and the gate rule that produced it, beside the check counts. Lead, retailer, agent, TL, call time and the pinned checklist version sit underneath — five seconds to know whether the sale should pass.
03 · Critical findingsEvidence-first

Observed against expected, with the line that proves it

Each finding separates what the transcript contained from what the authoritative source says — CRM field or retailer rate card — then the verbatim evidence, timestamp, confidence and rule version. The non-critical dead-air note is visibly a coaching note that does not block the sale.
04 · Evidence inspectorTraceability

Evidence, expectation and evaluation are kept apart

Opening any check — from a finding card, a checklist row or a timeline marker — gives the same four-part structure:

  • ▸Observed — what the transcript contains, at a word-level timestamp.
  • ▸Expected — the authoritative value, labelled with its source (CRM field, rate card, approved script).
  • ▸Evaluation — how the comparison was made and why it failed, including tolerance.
  • ▸Confidence and rule version — the check version that was live on the call date.

Playback states that card numbers are redacted in the stream. The panel closes with the product's own limit: it reports what failed and where; it does not rewrite the sale, correct the agent, or contact the customer.

05 · Call timelineSpeaker-separated · word-level timestamps

Where in the call it happened

Markers are positioned by their real offset in the call; failures and low-confidence checks render larger, dead air as a band. Selecting a marker opens that check's evidence and highlights the transcript turn — the thirty-minute listen becomes twenty seconds.
06 · Full checklistVerbatim · Factual · Behaviour

All twenty checks, grouped by type

Status carries a glyph and a word, never colour alone. Criticality, confidence, timestamp and evidence availability are on every row; any row opens its evidence. Tabs filter to the three check types from the brief.
07 · Human in the loopReview and lineage

The AI decision is never replaced

A reviewer agrees or overrides, with a required reason. Beside it, the decision lineage runs from lead creation through ingest, transcription, normalisation, the pinned rule set, execution, evidence, confidence and the gate.
07 · Override recordedRespect the override

Both decisions are retained

Confirming writes an audit event rather than changing the score: AI decision, human decision, reviewer, reason and time. The event appends to the ledger; the original gate decision stays exactly where it was.

The confirm action stays disabled until a reason is entered, so no override enters the record unexplained.

Override reasons feed the calibration view, where disagreement categories are tracked over time.

10 · State · ProcessingLead 3613830

Scoring in progress

The ingest pipeline is shown as itself: recording received, transcript generated, normalised, rule set pinned, checks executing, gate pending. The sale cannot submit while the gate has not run.
11 · State · Ingest errorLead 3613834

A failure holds the sale; it never passes by default

A zero-byte recording on the dialler callback means nothing was scored. The sale is held pending a usable artifact, with recovery actions: re-request the recording, or route to manual QA.
12 · Check libraryRetailer → checklist → version → effective date

Retailer policies are configuration, not code

Each retailer's checklist is versioned with an effective date, including superseded versions. Every check declares its type, criticality, weight and source of truth — which is what lets the same engine serve roughly thirty retailers.
13 · CalibrationSynthetic calibration data

Measuring the model, not trusting it

AI-to-auditor agreement alongside auditor-to-auditor agreement, the confidence distribution with the 0.85 auto-pass floor marked, and the categories where humans and the model disagree. Critical false-pass is the release-blocking metric.
14 · Audit ledgerAppend-only

Decision lineage, end to end

Every event carries a timestamp, an actor or system, the version in effect and the resulting state. Overrides add an event; they never rewrite the AI decision.
15 · AssumptionsWhere the brief was silent

Prototype assumptions, limits, and the next engineering step

Assumptions
  • 01Confidence floor of 0.85 for auto-pass; below that a check routes to QA.
  • 02Check weights of 10 (factual), 8 (verbatim), 3 (behaviour); the brief defines criticality, not weights.
  • 03Rate comparisons use a 0c tolerance; address and email use normalised string comparison.
  • 04Rule IDs, version numbers and effective dates are invented for the prototype.
  • 05Twenty checks stand in for Retailer 1's full energy checklist.
Limits & next step
  • ▸All metrics are synthetic. No CIMET production numbers appear.
  • ▸Audio playback is simulated; the redaction notice states the intended behaviour.
  • ▸The rule library is browsable but read-only in this pass.
  • ▸Frontend only — no ingest, ASR or scoring engine behind it.
